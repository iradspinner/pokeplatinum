# The principles behind Platinum Oxide's Pokemon changes

The rules as I would hand them to someone making the next change. Each is drawn from the changes already made and from Ian's answers of 2026-09-29 (in `rules.md`); the evidence and the exceptions are there, and `suggestions.md` shows them applied.

## The frame every rule serves

One capture per area rolls one ability, a hard cap per split makes the player fight with whatever stage a line is at, every fight is a first and only attempt, no item ever boosts damage past a type booster, and the player never controls the weather. A change is worth making when it gives a line an important niche at the point the player has it. Raising a line's ceiling is never a reason on its own. Some lines being stronger than others is fine. The goal is that every catch is a real choice.

## Sources, in order

1. Kaizo for stats and abilities. Take its number unless it exists to serve Kaizo's own design (its late evolution levels, its permanent weather and Trick Room areas, its boss gimmicks).
2. The later games for canon: the Fairy retypes and any official stat update.
3. Renegade Platinum for types. Its Complete-version retypes are the menu.
4. Pokemon Odyssey for a design idea when a line needs an identity rather than a number (the Plusle and Minun split).
5. Ian's own numbers only where none of the above has one, and then in round figures.

When two sources touch one Pokemon, stack them (Kaizo's stats plus Renegade's type on Flygon, Glalie, Luxray, Masquerain, Ninetales). Never split the difference between them.

## Stats

1. Buff the finished form into the 450 to 560 band, in proportion to the gap: about +45 for a final under 450, about +30 in the 450s and 460s, about +20 or nothing above 500. Round numbers, multiples of 5, mostly of 10.
2. Put the points where the Pokemon already attacks from, and let the other attacking stat pay for it. Take a reshuffle whole even when it flips an identity (Empoleon physical, Blastoise slow and special). A nerf is only ever the price of a bigger gain; never nerf on its own.
3. Bulk before Speed. Raise Speed only to a benchmark that changes a matchup, and accept a Speed drop when the design is a slow wall.
4. Buff a pre-evolution when the player will fight with it for a split or more under Oxide's own evolution levels: flat +10s to HP and the defences. Skip a source's pre-evo buff when its reason was that source's late evolution level.
5. Leave alone a line that already carries its split, a legendary, and any line you would not want to play. A change is an invitation to use the line.
6. Where two catches from the same grass are twins, make them two Pokemon: one a wall, one a sweeper, or one physical and one special.

## Types

1. Take every official retype. Beyond canon, add a type that names what the creature plainly is: Charizard and Sceptile are dragons, Luxray is a dark predator, Electivire is a brawler, Trapinch is a bug. If it does not excite you, do not add it.
2. Add, do not swap, unless the type being replaced is the line's weak point (Flying on Charizard and Flygon, Rock on Larvitar).
3. Change the whole line when the pre-evolution shows the trait; change only the final when it does not (Charmander has no wings, Shinx no darkness).
4. Prefer Dragon and Dark as additions. Reserve Fairy for canon and a favourite.
5. A type change may replace a stat buff when the line's problem is a matchup rather than a number (Larvitar and Pupitar through Fantina and Wake). It never replaces a bad ability, and a stat buff never replaces a bad type. Fix each kind of weakness in its own layer.

## Abilities

1. No regular slot holds a junk ability, because one capture rolls one ability. Junk means it does nothing in a singles battle for this player: Run Away, Pickup, Illuminate, Honey Gather, Stench, Klutz, Early Bird, Frisk, Forewarn, Anticipation, Own Tempo, Oblivious, Damp, Cute Charm, Normalize, Pressure, Unnerve, Healer, Hospitality, Tangled Feet, and anything that needs weather or terrain the player cannot set (Snow Cloak, Ice Body, Leaf Guard, Slush Rush, Flower Gift). Check the effect Oxide actually implements, not the Generation 4 one: Sturdy is junk only if it still blocks one-hit KO moves and nothing else.
2. Keep a vanilla ability that is genuinely useful beside the new one. Replace only the weak one.
3. Give a weak attacker a damage ability (Tinted Lens, Adaptability, Technician, Sniper, Super Luck, Reckless, Iron Fist, Scrappy, Hyper Cutter, Intimidate), a wall a damage-reducing one (Solid Rock, Filter, Thick Fat), and a special attacker Magic Guard.
4. No weather-setting or weather-cancelling ability in a regular slot of an obtainable line; move it to the hidden slot so trainers keep it and the single Ability Patch can reach it. Abilities that respond to weather (Swift Swim, Chlorophyll, Solar Power, Rain Dish, Hydration) may stay, because trainers keep their weather.
5. Refuse an ability that turns a fight into a foregone conclusion: Shadow Tag, Huge Power on a line built for it, Simple, Wonder Guard, Bad Dreams. Keep an added species' strongest modern hidden ability hidden (Protean, Libero, Contrary, Speed Boost) unless the regular slot is otherwise dead and the line is meant to sit a little above average.
6. A species' modern hidden ability may move into a regular slot when it is the line's identity and the slot it replaces is junk (Mamoswine's Thick Fat, Cinccino's Skill Link, Slowbro's Regenerator). Prefer this to inventing an ability the line never had.

## Checks before committing a change

1. Which split does the player first have this form in, under Oxide's evolution levels and stones? Judge the change there, not at level 100.
2. What niche does it open at that split that no other obtainable line fills? If none, do not make it.
3. Does the source number exist? Use it. If you must invent, round.
4. Does the line's pre-evolution now hold up for its stint? If the stint is long, buff the middle stage; do not touch the final to fix the middle.
5. Have you left both regular slots useful, and the weather in the hidden slot?
6. Does the change make a boss fight a foregone conclusion? If so, pull it back: the game is designed as first attempts.
7. Is the whole line consistent (types through the line where the trait shows; the same second ability on Sneasel and Weavile, on Hoothoot and Noctowl)?
8. Record what was taken and what was skipped, and why, so that a skip reads as a decision and not as a slip.
