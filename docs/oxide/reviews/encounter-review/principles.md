# The principles I used

These are the rules I found myself applying across the zones and the water, stated so that someone else can apply them to a new zone without reading the two reviews. They assume Ian's game: a nuzlocke with one capture per location name, a dupes clause, a player who reads the tables and picks the method, hard level caps, and tables whose shares and levels are settled.

## Fit

1. Start from the place, not the type. Write one sentence on what the zone is (a mine, a flower meadow, a fog stream, a bombed lake) and one on what vanilla put there; the table should be readable back from those two sentences. Where vanilla's list already says the place well (Gastly in the Chateau, Hippopotas in the dug tunnel, Sudowoodo on Route 221, Psyduck at the lakes), keep its signature or replace it with something that says the same thing louder.

2. A zone has one signature and it sits in the top slot. The 20% to 33% line is what the player remembers the zone by (Gible under the Cycling Road, Goomy in the flooded hall, Sandygast on the beach, Litwick in the foundry), so it must be the line that most belongs there, not the strongest available.

3. Each imported line has one home biome and at most one neighbouring zone. Later-generation species read as natives only when they arrive in the biome they came from (Frosmoth in the snow, Salandit on the volcano, Dewpider in the marsh, Toucannon on the islet). Used as filler across many zones (Nacli, Smoliv, Dubwool, Purrloin) they flatten the map, and a Galar sheep on a beach or a Paldean olive by a mountain stream is the first thing a fresh reader sees.

4. Filler slots draw from the zone's biome too. The 10% and 5% lines under the signature are what the player actually meets most, and they are where Oxide's caves and roads all became the same table. A cave's filler should differ from the last cave's (a mine has Nosepass and Magby, a ruin has Yamask and Klefki, a jewel cave has Carbink and Glimmet), and a road's filler should differ from the last road's.

5. Keep the region's own animals in the region. Sinnoh's bird is Starly and it should evolve somewhere; Bidoof should reach Bibarel; Psyduck and Golduck are the lakes' residents; Kricketot, Chingling, Chatot, Girafarig and the Bronzor line are what makes Sinnoh look like Sinnoh. A native at its vanilla share, or its adult in a late zone, is always the safe replacement for a filler import.

6. A leftover is a species with no story in its slot. Test each line by asking what it is doing there: a Seel on a dry road, a Slugma in a blizzard, a Sandygast underground, a Wailmer in a stream, a Chatot in the ash, a Raichu on a sea islet, a Grapploct up a mountain. If there is no answer, it is a leftover and the slot is open.

7. Night and day slots are the cheapest place to add a line and the easiest place to lose one. They should bring in a line the main table does not have (an owl, a crow, a ghost), and the same night line should not follow the player through the whole game; Purrloin's twenty-three zones came from treating the night slot as a default.

## Growth across the run

8. Every split should introduce lines the player has not seen. After Byron, Oxide introduces three; the last third of the game is evolved forms of the first two thirds, and under the dupes clause a full box makes most of it already owned. Late zones should carry the adult or the rarer relative of a placed line (Staraptor, Noctowl, Machamp, Hippowdon, Dusclops, Chimecho, Crobat, Gabite) or a line held back for the purpose, and a designer should keep a list of which lines are still unseen by each split.

9. A type may be thin, but not absent for three splits. Fairy is absent from Byron's land and nearly absent from Wake's; the check is a per-split type count of the land tables, weighted by share, read for zeros rather than for balance.

10. One capture area, one table's worth of variety. Where several maps share a name (the Lost Tower's floors, the Great Marsh's six areas, the Coronet halls), their differences are for the walk, not the catch. Judge the area by the union of its lines, and spend design effort on areas that are separate captures.

## Water

11. Water has biomes, and each zone gets one: village pond, lowland river, mountain stream, cave pool, lake, marsh, industrial water, hot spring or oasis, beach and shallows, harbour, open sea, cold sea. A zone's four tables draw from its biome's palette and nothing else. This one rule removes most of the repetition: a deep-sea anglerfish (Chinchou) cannot then be in thirty-six zones, and a shark cannot be in a village pond.

12. Sea species stay in the sea. Chinchou and Lanturn, Sharpedo, Wailmer, Mantyke, Tentacool, Corsola, Clamperl, Luvdisc, Remoraid, Finneon, Horsea, Qwilfish, Staryu, Shellder and Relicanth belong to coast, harbour and open sea. Inland water uses the freshwater palette (Goldeen, Barboach, Corphish, Poliwag, Wooper, Lotad, Surskit, Psyduck, Buizel, Marill, Slowpoke, Carvanha, Feebas, Goomy, the Zubat line over caves). The exceptions are earned by the place: an electric fish at the power plant, a Lapras in the cold lake.

13. The four tables are four positions, not four ages. Old Rod: the shallows, fry and small first stages. Good Rod: mid-water, crustaceans, mid-sized fish. Super Rod: the deep, one adult prize and its company. Surf: the surface, what floats, skates, wades or flies over the water. Vanilla's rods were the same line at three sizes (Magikarp, then Magikarp and Goldeen, then Gyarados and Seaking); that is the pattern to avoid.

14. Same line, one table. Within a zone a line appears in one of the four tables only, with one exception: the adult of a Good Rod first stage may be the Super Rod's 1%. This triples what a zone offers a dupes-clause player and is the direct cure for the Super Rod being the Good Rod grown up.

15. Non-Water species go on the water where the habitat is documented, and mostly on the Surf. The precedents: vanilla's Zubat and Golbat over the Gate, Ravaged Path and Coronet water; Meteor Falls' Zubat, Golbat, Solrock and Lunatone; Grimer in Celadon's pond; Dratini by rod on Kalos Route 21; Dhelmise by rod at Seafolk Village. In Oxide's own tables, Drifloon over the Windworks, Goomy in damp water, Dhelmise on a sea rod, Golbat leading a cave's Surf. Keep non-Water at 30% or below except in caves, where the bat can lead as vanilla had it.

16. A top pair belongs to one zone per split. No two zones whose tables open in the same split share both their 60/30 or 40/40 lines. Check this per split, not per zone, because the Good Rod opens sixteen tables at once and the Super Rod thirty-eight.

17. The prize sits where the place is about water. The sea routes and the lakes carry the 500-plus adults (Lapras, Kingdra, Wailord, Gorebyss, Mantine, Tentacruel, Milotic, Golduck, Poliwrath, Walrein). A river or a canal carries small freshwater life whose adults are 430 to 480, so a river never out-bids its own grass at the same split and a deferred river gives a good body rather than the best one.

18. Filler is varied filler. A deliberately shallow table (a town canal, a cave puddle) still holds five lines from five families, so that under the dupes clause it never collapses to a single species, and neighbouring shallow tables in the same split use different fives.

19. The 1% is the place's rarity, not a running gag. Each zone's rare slot is a thing that lives there and is worth the roll; a single line as the 1% of thirty-five zones is a joke told thirty-five times and, under the dupes clause, the slot every owned table collapses to.

20. Make deferral a choice. Where a player will save a weak early zone for its late water, the early zones' Super Rod tables should each offer a different deep-water adult, so that saving five zones yields five lines. The strategic layer stays; the shopping list goes.

## Method

21. Count distinct lines per capture area across every method, land included. Aim for at least eight in a water-only town and at least twelve in a route with both; below that, a dupes-clause player has nothing to catch on a second visit.

22. Count each line's zones across the whole map. Anything over ten zones is filler by definition and should be cut to one biome and its neighbours; anything under two zones that is not a set piece is a prize and should be placed on purpose.

23. Swap within worth. A replacement keeps the slot's share and level and is the same stage, evolves at a similar level, and reaches a similar final form by the split's cap; when in doubt, the weaker of the two is the safe direction, and a raised ceiling (a 1% Gabite in Victory Road) is called out as such.

24. Prefer the native, then the placed line, then the flag. When a slot is open, vanilla's resident at its vanilla share is the first candidate, a placed line from the biome the second, and an unplaced line is written down for the designer rather than proposed.
