# Wild encounter review, part two: the water tables

The water tables are entirely Water-typed, repeat a handful of lines across the whole map, and make the four tables of a zone the same few lines at four ages. Under Ian's habits (he reads the tables, picks the method, and often saves an early zone for its late water) that turns about a dozen early zones into one shopping list drawn from the same three or four species. Water is not stronger than the late land tables; the pull is that the early land tables are weak and the deferred water is the same everywhere. This file diagnoses that, sets out the principles that answer it, and applies them zone by zone within the current balance.

## Questions asked, and Ian's answers

1. Unplaced lines: only species already in a wild table may be proposed; a notable line that fits is flagged.
2. Method: Ian reads the tables and picks grass, rod or Surf beforehand.
3. Deferral: often; the late water tables of early zones are real options.
4. Non-Water in water: yes, anywhere the habitat case is solid.
5. Water places: the sea routes (219, 220, 223, 218, 221, 222, 226, 230) and the three lakes with Sendoff Spring; not the Great Marsh, not the route rivers.
6. Route 218's Galarian Mr. Mime: a leftover, propose freely.
7. Shallow filler tables: yes, if the filler stays varied for the dupes clause.
8. Ian's water captures so far: mostly deferred Super Rod and Surf picks.

## 1. Diagnosis

### What is there

Forty-three capture areas have water; between them they hold 203 tables (51 Old Rod, 51 Good Rod, 51 Super Rod, 50 Surf), because Route 204, Route 205 and Route 212 have two maps each and the Great Marsh six. Every table but three is 100% Water-typed by share; the three are Snowpoint's rods, where Snorunt (1%), Snover (4%) and Froslass (4%) sit among the seals. Dual types vary the picture (Water/Ground, Water/Electric, Water/Ghost, Water/Grass, Water/Bug, Water/Dark, Water/Poison, Water/Flying, Water/Rock, Water/Ice, Water/Psychic, Water/Fighting, Water/Dragon), but every fish, frog, crab and jelly resists Fire, Ice, Steel and Water, and every one of them is what the player already has once he owns two of them. The whole water system contains 41 lines; the land tables contain about 150.

### Repetition inside a zone

A typical zone's four tables hold twenty slots and twelve distinct lines. The overlap is not random: the Super Rod is usually the Good Rod grown up (Goldeen and Barboach on the rods, Seaking and Whiscash on the Super Rod; Corphish on the Good Rod, Crawdaunt on the Super Rod), so a player who fished the zone once has nothing new in its deeper table. Route 210 north has the Goldeen line in the top pair of three of its four tables; Mt. Coronet South has Goldeen and Barboach leading both rods and Whiscash leading the third; Twinleaf has the Corphish line in three of its four tables. The Froakie line is the 1% in thirty-five zones, which reads as one joke told thirty-five times.

### By split

At every split the water is entirely Water-typed, so the question is what it offers against the land at the moment the player can use it. In Roark's split the Old Rod gives nineteen lines across seven zones (Twinleaf, Lake Verity, Route 203, Route 204, Ravaged Path, Route 219 and, from Jubilife, Route 218), and those first casts are varied and well pitched (Goldeen and Barboach inland, Tentacool and Finneon at the beach, Finneon and Krabby in the strait). The trouble starts when the later tables open. At Maylene's split sixteen Good Rod tables open at once, all Lv 15-28 against a cap of 38, and eight of them are Goldeen, Barboach and Corphish again. At Byron's split thirty-five Surf tables open; sixteen lead with the Buizel or Shellos lines. At Candice's split thirty-eight Super Rod tables open at Lv 30-44 against a cap of 56, and fourteen are Whiscash tables.

### Does water beat land?

Not in raw strength. The best late land tables lead with Metang, Kirlia, Jangmo-o, Goomy, Absol and Steelix at 20% to 33%, lines that reach 530 to 605; the water's ceiling is Milotic, Kingdra, Wailord and Walrein (540 to 555) and its typical 40% adult is 450 to 480. What water does is offer a Lv 30-42 adult of 460 to a player whose early land captures were Wooloo, Blipbug, Sentret and Purrloin at cap 16. Since Ian saves early zones for later water, the zones that reward deferral are those with weak grass and water: Ravaged Path (Zubat and Geodude on land, Milotic at 40% on the Super Rod), Lake Verity, Route 203, Oreburgh Gate, Route 204, Route 205, Eterna City and Twinleaf (no grass). That is a real and good strategic layer. The fault is that all eight pay out from the same pool: Milotic first (super-wanted and fixed, so the near-guarantee is by design), then Whiscash, Crawdaunt, Lanturn and Sharpedo, then, once those are owned, the dupes clause reduces each table to its 4% and 1% slots (Golduck, Poliwrath, Greninja). So the "best route uses water wherever possible" pattern is present in a mild form, and the cure is not to weaken water but to make the deferred tables differ.

The twelve zones whose only capture is water (Twinleaf, Eterna, Celestic, Pastoria, Canalave, Snowpoint, the Resort Area, Sunyshore, the League, Routes 219, 220 and 223) are a separate case: they cannot fall back to grass, so their variety has to be their own. Snowpoint (six lines across three tables) and Pastoria (nine) are the thinnest; the League's four tables are the Coronet and lake list on a mountaintop and are the one water-only zone that does not read as its place.

## 2. Principles

The full set, with the land rules, is in `principles.md`; these are the water ones.

Water has biomes, and each zone gets one. Sinnoh's water divides cleanly into a village pond, a lowland river, a mountain stream, a cave pool, a lake, a marsh, industrial water (Fuego, the Windworks), a hot spring or oasis (Routes 227 and 228), a beach with shallows, a harbour, the open sea and a cold sea. A zone's four tables draw from its biome's palette and nothing else, which by itself removes Chinchou from thirty of its thirty-six zones and Sharpedo from every pond. The palettes, using only placed lines:

- Pond and lowland river: Goldeen, Barboach, Corphish, Poliwag, Wooper, Lotad, Surskit, Psyduck, Buizel, Marill, Slowpoke, Carvanha, Shellos, Bidoof's line (flag), Dratini (flag).
- Mountain stream and cave pool: Barboach, Goldeen, Poliwag, Feebas where fixed, Corphish, Goomy, Frillish in haunted water, and Zubat and Golbat over the water as vanilla has them.
- Lake: Psyduck and Golduck, Goldeen, Surskit, Lotad, Marill, Slowpoke, Feebas where fixed, Frillish at the bombed and fogbound lakes, Lapras at Acuity, Seel and Dewgong at the cold lake.
- Marsh: Wooper, Lotad, Surskit, Dewpider, Carvanha, Slowpoke, Barboach, Krabby, Frogadier (its only proper home), Yanma, Croagunk and Goomy over the water.
- Beach and shallows: Tentacool, Wingull, Shellos, Corsola, Staryu, Krabby, Finneon, Remoraid, Luvdisc, Mareanie, Chinchou, Mantyke, Horsea, Qwilfish, Clamperl.
- Harbour and open sea: Tentacruel, Mantine, Wailmer, Sharpedo, Lanturn, Kingdra, Lumineon, Octillery, Relicanth, Gorebyss, Jellicent, Lapras, Pelipper, Shellder, Dhelmise.
- Cold sea: Seel, Spheal, Lapras, Shellder, Relicanth, with Snorunt's line as Oxide already has it.
- Industrial: Chinchou and Lanturn at the Windworks (the one inland place an electric fish belongs), Qwilfish, Shellos and Gastrodon at Fuego, Drifloon over the Windworks, Grimer at Fuego (flag).

The four tables are four positions, not four ages. The Old Rod is the shallows: fry and small first stages. The Good Rod is mid-water: crustaceans, mid-sized fish, first stages that evolve soon. The Super Rod is the deep: one adult prize and its company. Surf is the surface: what floats, skates, wades or flies over the water. That last is where non-Water species belong, and it is where vanilla put them (Zubat and Golbat over the Gate, Ravaged Path and Coronet water; Meteor Falls' Zubat, Golbat, Solrock and Lunatone in Hoenn) and where the later games put Grimer (HeartGold's Celadon pond) and, by rod, Dratini (Kalos Route 21) and Dhelmise (Alola's Seafolk Village).

Same line, one table. Within a zone a line appears in at most one of the four tables, with one allowed exception: the adult of a Good Rod first stage may be the Super Rod's 1%. This is the direct cure for the Super Rod being the Good Rod grown up, and it triples what a zone offers a dupes-clause player.

A top pair belongs to one zone per split. No two zones whose tables open in the same split share both their 60/30 or 40/40 lines.

Sea species stay in the sea. Chinchou and Lanturn, Sharpedo, Wailmer, Mantyke, Tentacool, Corsola, Clamperl, Luvdisc, Remoraid, Finneon, Horsea, Qwilfish, Staryu, Shellder and Relicanth are coast, harbour and open-sea lines, with two inland exceptions: Chinchou at the Windworks, and Lapras at Lake Acuity. Inland water uses the freshwater palette, and Psyduck's line, vanilla's lake and river standard, returns to the 30% and 40% slots it held.

Non-Water goes where the habitat is documented, and mostly on the Surf. Golbat can lead a cave's Surf at 60% as vanilla's Coronet did (Zubat 90%); elsewhere non-Water stays at 30% and below.

The prize sits where the place is about water. Ian named the sea routes and the lakes with Sendoff Spring. Those carry the 500-plus adults (Lapras, Kingdra, Wailord, Gorebyss, Mantine, Tentacruel, Milotic where fixed, Golduck, Poliwrath, Walrein). A river or a town canal carries small freshwater life whose adults are 430 to 480, so a river never out-bids its grass at the same split, and a deferred river gives a good body, not the best body.

Filler is varied filler. A shallow table still holds five lines from five families, so that it never collapses to one species under the dupes clause, and neighbouring shallow tables in the same split use different fives.

The 1% is the place's rarity, not a running gag. Each zone's rare slot is something that lives there: Drifloon over the Windworks, a Frillish beside the Lost Tower's stream, a Kingdra off Route 223, a Lapras off Iron Island, Dhelmise on the Route 221 rod. The Froakie line keeps Pastoria, the marsh and Route 212, where the frog lives.

Make deferral a choice. The early zones' Super Rod tables should each offer a different deep-water adult (Golduck here, Poliwrath there, Slowbro, Whiscash, Crawdaunt, Ludicolo, Gastrodon, Seaking, Quagsire, Kingler, Azumarill), so that saving five zones for Candice yields five different lines rather than five rolls at the same three.

### What the proposals do in total

Chinchou's line falls from thirty-six zones to the sea routes, the harbours, the Windworks and the lake water where it was a rare slot; the Froakie line from thirty-five zones to Pastoria, the marsh and Route 212; Sharpedo, Wailmer, Horsea, Clamperl, Corsola, Luvdisc and Mantyke leave every inland pond. Psyduck and Golduck return to vanilla's lakes, the Gate and Sendoff Spring; Golbat is over the water in every cave that had it; Drifloon, Goomy, Dhelmise and the Zubat line are the non-Water surface life, with Dratini, Grimer and the Magikarp line flagged for Ian. The early zones' Super Rod tables now pay out Golduck, Poliwrath, Quagsire, Whiscash, Crawdaunt, Slowbro, Ludicolo, Kingler and Gastrodon in different zones rather than Whiscash and Lanturn in all of them, so deferring five zones gives five lines.
