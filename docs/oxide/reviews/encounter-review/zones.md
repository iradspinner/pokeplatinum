# Wild encounter review, part one: the zones

A fresh reader's judgement of each main-game zone's wild table: whether the list fits the place, reads as a theme, and sits right in the run of zones around it, with proposals that keep every table's shares and levels. The water tables have their own review in `water.md`; here each water zone gets its place and fit, and its water proposals are only summarised. The rules I applied are collected in `principles.md`.

## Questions asked, and Ian's answers

1. May I propose lines that have no wild table at all (Aron, Oddish, Growlithe, Dratini, Grimer, Bibarel, the Magikarp line and the rest)? Only species already in a wild table; a particularly notable species or line that fits should be flagged for review, not proposed.
2. How is an area's one capture taken when it has both grass and water? Ian reads the tables and picks the method beforehand.
3. Does Ian leave an early area's capture unused to come back with a better rod or Surf? Often; the late water tables of early zones are real options.
4. May non-Water Pokemon enter rod and Surf tables where the habitat case is solid? Yes, anywhere the case is solid.
5. Which areas are the run's water places, where a water capture is the intended prize? The sea routes (219, 220, 223, 218, 221, 222, 226, 230) and the three lakes with Sendoff Spring. Not the Great Marsh, and not the rivers on ordinary routes.
6. Is Galarian Mr. Mime, the 28% top slot on Route 218, meant as that route's signature? No, a leftover; propose freely.
7. May some water tables be deliberately shallow filler so the strong water captures concentrate where the place is about water? Yes, but the filler must stay varied, because the dupes clause turns a table of owned lines into its rare slots.
8. Which water captures has Ian taken or planned in his run? Mostly deferred Super Rod and Surf picks.

## How to read the proposals

Each proposal names the table and the slot share it touches, and swaps one species for another of about the same worth at that point: same stage, a similar evolution level, and a comparable final form by the split's cap. Replacements come from lines that already have a wild table (answer 1); where an evolved form of a placed line is the natural stage for a late table, I use it and say so. Ian's super-wanted lines are never touched, which means Vulpix, Ponyta, Budew, Bounsweet, Buneary, Rookidee, Swablu, Ralts, Koffing, Skitty, Skorupi, Snorunt, Feebas, Alolan Ninetales, Galarian Rapidash and Galarian Weezing stay where they are even where I would otherwise have moved them. A line I think belongs in a place but has no wild table is listed as a flag, not a proposal. Vanilla Platinum's tables are taken from `zones.md`; terrain comes from Serebii's Pokearth pages (Bulbapedia refused automated reading), habitat from the FireRed and LeafGreen habitat data served by PokeAPI, and later placements from Serebii's pages for those games.

## Roark split (level cap 16)

### Twinleaf Town

The place: the player's home town, a few houses at the southern tip of Sinnoh with a pond at its edge that can be fished from the first day and surfed much later. In the games' world it is quiet water with nothing dramatic in it.

Fit: the Old Rod (Goldeen, Barboach, Corphish, Squirtle, Buizel) reads exactly like a village pond and is a fine first cast. The Super Rod, which Ian will use here after Candice because the town has no grass, holds Lanturn and Sharpedo, a deep-sea anglerfish and a shark, in that same pond; the Surf table's Buizel, Lombre and Masquerain fit. See `water.md` for the proposals (Golduck and Quagsire in place of the two sea species, Poliwag in place of Chinchou on the Good Rod).

Confidence: high on the fit, medium on the replacements.

### Route 201

The place: the first road, flat grass between Twinleaf and Sandgem with the lakefront branching off it. Vanilla's Starly, Bidoof and Kricketot are the baseline "first route" cast.

Fit: the table is a good first route: a sheep, a ferret, two birds, a beetle, a cat, a kitten cub and the Nidoran pair, with the day and night slots doing real work. It reads as meadow. Two things carry forward across the run rather than jar here: Wooloo leads at 20% and turns up in nine zones, and Purrloin's night slot is the first of twenty-three zones for that line (counting Liepard), which is where the run's Dark filler comes from.

Proposals:
- Night: Purrloin out, Hoothoot in. Sinnoh's night bird is Hoothoot, it is already placed (Route 211), it is about as strong (262 against 281), and this starts trimming Purrloin's twenty-three zones.

Confidence: high.

### Verity Lakefront

The place: the grassy approach to Lake Verity from Route 201, where the story opens. Vanilla has no table here; Oxide added one.

Fit: Starly, Blipbug, Wooloo, Purrloin, Sentret, Surskit, Buneary and Vulpix, with Pikipek and Minccino by day, read as lakeside meadow; Surskit on the land table is the right lakeside touch. It overlaps Route 201 heavily (Starly, Blipbug, Wooloo, Purrloin, Sentret), which under the dupes clause means a Route 201 capture removes most of this table's weight. That is not wrong for two adjacent meadows, but it makes the lakefront a weaker second capture than it looks.

Proposals:
- Night: Purrloin out, Poochyena in. Poochyena is placed (Sandgem, Jubilife), the same stage and strength, and it gives the lakefront a night line Route 201 does not have.

Confidence: medium.

### Lake Verity

The place: the southern of the three great lakes, a cave-type map with a shore of grass inside a ring of cliffs; Mesprit lives under it. In vanilla the water is Psyduck and Golduck by Surf and Goldeen and Magikarp by rod.

Fit: the land table (Surskit 25%, Bidoof, Wooloo, Lotad, Pikipek, Blipbug, Buneary, Krabby) is good lakeside, and Bidoof is the right Sinnoh note by a lake. Krabby at 1% is a sea crab in a mountain lake. The water tables are the lake's real capture and are reviewed in `water.md`: the Old Rod (Surskit, Goldeen, Chinchou, Mudkip, Psyduck) is fine; the Super Rod's Lanturn and Sharpedo are sea animals again.

Proposals:
- Grass 1%: Krabby out, Psyduck in. Psyduck is the vanilla resident of this lake, is placed, and at 1% it is the same kind of rare shore find (320 against 325).
- Night: Purrloin out, Hoothoot (as Route 201; the lake has an owl in vanilla's own Sinnoh night lists) in.
- Water: see `water.md` (Golduck and Poliwrath in place of Lanturn and Sharpedo on the Super Rod, Poliwag and Marill in the small Chinchou slots).

Confidence: high.

### Sandgem Town

The place: the seaside town with Rowan's lab, sand to the south where Route 219's beach begins. Vanilla has no table.

Fit: Kricketot, Bidoof, Starly, Wurmple, Blipbug, Wooloo, Sentret, Rookidee, Wingull, Pikipek, Poochyena and Abra read as the edge of a town by the sea; Wingull at 4% is the one seaside note and is right. Nothing jars.

Proposals:
- Night: Purrloin out, Hoothoot (the Purrloin thinning; Kricketot stays) in.

Confidence: high.

### Route 202

The place: the short grass road north from Sandgem to Jubilife, vanilla's Shinx route.

Fit: Shinx leads at 20% as in vanilla, with Blipbug, Wooloo, Bidoof, Starly, Purrloin, Grubbin, Rookidee, the Nidorans, Budew and Smoliv, Sewaddle and Pikipek by day. It reads as a hedgerow road and is the fourth table in a row drawing from the same eight lines, which is fine for the first hour of a game but is where I would want the first surprise. Smoliv (an olive from Paldea's Mediterranean coast) at 1% is a curiosity here and turns up in fourteen zones.

Proposals:
- Night: Purrloin out, Hoothoot in.
- 1%: Smoliv out, Kricketot in. Kricketot is the vanilla resident and only a night slot in Oxide; a 1% day-time Kricketot keeps the cricket in its route without touching the Smoliv line elsewhere.

Confidence: medium.

### Jubilife City

The place: Sinnoh's largest city, with grass patches in its parks. Vanilla has no wild table.

Fit: Abra and Machop at 20% each, Minccino, Pawmi, Rookidee, Shinx, Starly, Purrloin, Glameow, Poochyena, Skitty and Murkrow, with Murkrow and Abra at night: an urban table in the FireRed habitat sense (Abra, Machop's line, Glameow, Murkrow are all "urban" there), and the best-themed table in the split. Nothing jars.

Proposals:
- Night: Purrloin out, Murkrow in. Murkrow is already the city's 1% and is the right night bird for a city; Purrloin stays in the day table.

Confidence: high.

### Route 203

The place: the road east from Jubilife to the Oreburgh Gate, with a small fishing pool. Vanilla: Starly, Shinx, Bidoof, Abra, Kricketot.

Fit: Rookidee 20%, Nidoran pair 20% each, Purrloin, Shinx, Makuhita, Abra, Fletchling: a road that is already turning towards the mountains, which is what Makuhita says. Good. The pool's water is reviewed in `water.md` (Wingull and the sea fish out, Psyduck and Poliwag in).

Proposals:
- Night: Purrloin out, Hoothoot (Nidoran♀ stays) in.

Confidence: high.

### Oreburgh Gate

The place: the tunnel through the ridge into Oreburgh, two floors, with a pool on the lower floor reached by Rock Smash and Surf. Vanilla: Zubat, Psyduck, Geodude on 1F; Zubat, Psyduck, Geodude, Golbat below, Psyduck and Zubat on the water.

Fit: 1F (Zubat, Nacli, Makuhita, Nosepass, Nidoran♂, Purrloin, Onix, Gligar; Geodude at night) reads as a cave mouth. B1F (Geodude 28%, Phanpy, Rhyhorn, Dwebble, Glimmet, Carbink, Mudkip, Nacli, Onix) is a good rock-and-tusk cellar and Mudkip at 5% by the pool is a nice touch. Purrloin at 10% inside a cave is the one jar; Psyduck, the vanilla Gate's whole personality, is gone from the land and is a 1% on the Surf table.

Proposals:
- 1F 10%: Purrloin out, Geodude in. Geodude is only a night slot here and vanilla's Gate is 15% Geodude; same stage, about as strong (300 against 281).
- Water: see `water.md` (the vanilla Surf with Psyduck and Zubat restored, Whiscash on the Super Rod, Clamperl and Wailmer out of a cave pool).

Confidence: high.

### Route 207

The place: the rocky rise from Oreburgh towards Mt. Coronet and the Cycling Road, a mountain background with a small patch of grass. Vanilla: Machop, Geodude, Ponyta, Kricketot.

Fit: Ponyta and Vulpix at 20% each, Machop, Nacli, Geodude, Fletchling, Houndour, Smoliv, Makuhita, Phanpy, with Charmander and Fennekin at 1%: a sun-baked slope, coherent and a real change from the meadows below. Fine.

Proposals: none.

Confidence: high.

### Oreburgh Mine

The place: the working coal mine under Oreburgh, where Roark is found. Vanilla: Geodude, Zubat, Onix.

Fit: Makuhita 25%, Onix 20%, Phanpy, Nacli, Geodude, Nosepass, Rhyhorn, Magby 1%; Zubat only by day. It reads as a mine (Onix, Nosepass and a Magby by the furnaces), and Makuhita as the miners' Pokemon works. The only note is that it shares most of its lines with the Gate and Ravaged Path, so as the third cave in the split its capture is often a dupe of the first.

Proposals: none.

Confidence: high.

### Route 204

The place: the road north from Jubilife to Floaroma, split by the Ravaged Path into a southern meadow with a pond and a northern flowered stretch; both halves are one capture. Vanilla: Starly, Bidoof, Budew, Shinx, Wurmple, Kricketot.

Fit: the south (Budew 20%, Grubbin 20%, Fletchling, Wurmple, Wooloo, Sewaddle, Bounsweet, Shroomish, Lotad, Seedot, Smoliv, Snivy) is the first green table and reads as it should. The north, reached at Gardenia's split, is starters and their kin (Sewaddle 28%, Litten 20%, Snivy 15%, Bounsweet, Treecko, Budew, Snubbull, Torchic by day): coherent as "the route past the forest" and a deliberate Oxide feature (starter lines as wild lines), though Litten leading a Sinnoh flower route at 20% is the first place a fresh reader notices the region has changed. Water in `water.md`.

Proposals:
- South night: Purrloin out, Hoothoot (Seedot stays) in.

Confidence: medium.

### Ravaged Path

The place: the short cave splitting Route 204, with a pool in it reached by Surf. Vanilla: Zubat and Psyduck on land, Psyduck and Zubat on the water.

Fit: Zubat, Geodude, Makuhita, Nacli, Phanpy, Nosepass, Onix, Lotad. Lotad by the pool is right. It is the same cave as the Gate and the Mine with the numbers shuffled, and it is the third such table inside five zones. Its water is a bigger matter: the Super Rod holds Milotic at 40% (super-wanted, stays), so a deferred Ravaged Path capture is one of the run's obvious moves; `water.md` proposes restoring Psyduck and Golbat to the Surf and taking the sea fish out of the pool.

Proposals: none on land.

Confidence: high.

### Route 219

The place: the beach south of Sandgem, no grass at all, a short strip of shallow sea that leads to Route 220. Vanilla: Tentacool, Wingull, Tentacruel and Pelipper by Surf, Finneon and Lumineon by rod.

Fit: the Old Rod (Tentacool 60%, Finneon, Luvdisc, Froakie, Remoraid) is a sea table available in the first hour, which is a good contrast to the ponds inland; the Surf table (Wingull 60%, Mantyke, Gastrodon, Tentacool) is vanilla's beach with a ray. Froakie on a sea rod is the odd one. Proposals in `water.md`.

Confidence: high.

## Gardenia split (level cap 26)

### Floaroma Town

The place: the flower town, a meadow of blossoms with the honey seller and the Windworks road. No vanilla table.

Fit: Combee and Cherubi at 20% each, Budew, Bounsweet, Tropius, Poochyena, Pachirisu, Aipom, Hoppip, Chingling, Skitty; Murkrow at night. Honey, blossom, fruit: the best-fitting town table in the game. Tropius (a fruit-bearing tree Pokemon) at 10% on a flower meadow is an Oxide choice a fresh reader accepts at once.

Proposals: none.

Confidence: high.

### Route 205

The place: the long river route around Floaroma, south of the Windworks and north of Eterna Forest; one capture across both halves. Vanilla: Shellos, Buizel, Bidoof, Pachirisu in the south; Bidoof, Budew, Wurmple and its cocoons in the north.

Fit: the south (Pachirisu 20%, Smoliv 20%, Minccino, Aipom, Mareep, Fletchling, Pawmi, Bounsweet, Sewaddle, Combee, Pikachu) reads as meadow by a power plant, with the Electric rodents doing the Windworks' work; Smoliv at 20% is a Paldean olive grove where vanilla had a beaver. The north (Lotad 20%, Cherubi 20%, Sewaddle, Hoppip, Shroomish, Wooper, Buizel, Marill, Dewpider, Froakie, Shellos; Surskit and Wooper at night) is the best riverside table in the game. Water in `water.md`.

Proposals:
- South 20%: Smoliv out, Bidoof in. Bidoof is vanilla's Route 205 resident, is placed (Route 202, Sandgem, Lake Verity), evolves at 15 into a 410 Bibarel where Smoliv reaches a 354 Dolliv by the cap; the beaver belongs on a river and Smoliv keeps thirteen other zones.

Confidence: medium.

### Valley Windworks

The place: the small power plant on its own spit of grass east of Route 205, taken over by Galactic; the Drifloon appears at its gate on Fridays. Vanilla: Shellos, Buizel, Shinx, Pachirisu.

Fit: Pawmi 28%, Shinx 25%, Pachirisu, Minccino, Minun, Plusle, Emolga 1%, Pikachu 1%: the Electric theme is exactly right for the place and unmistakable. Pikipek and Rookidee by day are fine. The one thing missing is the plant's own ghost, Drifloon, which `water.md` proposes putting over the water.

Proposals: none on land.

Confidence: high.

### Eterna Forest

The place: the dark wood between Route 205 and Eterna, with the Old Chateau at its far end and a mossy rock. Vanilla: Budew, Buneary, Wurmple's line, Kricketot, Bidoof, Gastly.

Fit: Shroomish 23%, Buneary 20%, Seedot 20%, Sewaddle, Beautifly, Burmy, Combee, Steenee; Rowlet and Budew by day, Seedot and Murkrow at night. Mushrooms, acorns, moths and an owl: this is a forest, and moving Gastly wholesale into the Chateau next door is the right call. Nothing jars.

Proposals: none.

Confidence: high.

### Eterna City

The place: the old city under the statue of the dragons, with a canal to fish and, later, surf. Water only; see `water.md`. The Old Rod (Goldeen, Lotad, Shellos, Barboach, Froakie) is honest canal filler and fine; the Super Rod's Lanturn, Sharpedo and Alomomola are all ocean animals in a city canal.

Confidence: high.

### Route 211

The place: the mountain road from Eterna up the west face of Mt. Coronet, cut in two by the mountain, with a stream coming down it. The west half is Gardenia-split, the east half is reached from Celestic at Byron's split. Vanilla: Meditite, Chingling, Machop, Bronzor, Bidoof.

Fit: the west (Alolan Ninetales 28% at Lv 13-16, Mienfoo, Vulpix, Bronzor, Snover, Chingling, Charmander, Zubat, Meditite; Ponyta and Rookidee by day, Alolan Ninetales and Hoothoot at night) is Oxide's first cold-mountain table and a super-wanted showcase; Snover and Bronzor say "Coronet" correctly. The east (Kirlia 28%, Jangmo-o 21%, Ferroseed, Turtonator, Dartrix, Gligar; Naclstack and Sneasel at night) reads as high mountain with a dragon in it, which is right, except Turtonator, a Fire/Dragon turtle from a volcano, at 15% on a fog-and-stream mountainside.

Proposals:
- East 15%: Turtonator out, Graveler in. Vanilla's Route 211 east is 20% Graveler; the Geodude line is placed everywhere, Graveler at Lv 28-29 is the natural stage, and it is about as strong a capture at cap 53 (Graveler 430 evolving on) as a 485 Turtonator. Turtonator keeps Stark Mountain and Route 227, where it belongs.

Confidence: high.

### Mt. Coronet North

The place: the first Coronet room the player crosses, from Route 211 towards Route 208, a cold stone hall. Vanilla: Bronzor, Geodude, Meditite, Cleffa, Machop, Chingling, Nosepass, Zubat.

Fit: Bronzor 20%, Clefairy 20%, Zubat, Snorunt, Alolan Ninetales, Carbink, Snover, Nosepass, Swinub, Makuhita, Phanpy. Clefairy and Bronzor keep vanilla's Coronet identity, Snorunt and Swinub bring the cold down early, Carbink is a good jewel-cave addition. Fits.

Proposals: none.

Confidence: high.

## Fantina split (level cap 33)

### Old Chateau

The place: the abandoned mansion at the end of Eterna Forest, haunted, with Rotom in its television. Vanilla: Gastly only, on every floor.

Fit: Gastly 33%, Litwick 30%, Misdreavus, Sinistea, Yamask; Sinistea and Litwick by day, Gastly and Duskull at night. Candles, teacups, a mask: it reads as the inside of a haunted house, and giving it five ghosts where vanilla had one is the right expansion. It shares Gastly, Litwick, Misdreavus, Sinistea and Duskull with the Lost Tower, which is a separate capture, so a Chateau capture leaves the Tower with Yamask, Koffing, Gothita, Drifloon, Klefki, Dhelmise and Sandygast: still plenty. Nothing jars. Rotom is the vanilla set piece and rightly not in the table.

Proposals: none.

Confidence: high.

### Route 206

The place: the Cycling Road on its stilts above the valley, with the grass and Wayward Cave underneath; dry, rocky, no water. Vanilla: Geodude, Ponyta, Gligar, Machop, Kricketune.

Fit: Gligar 20%, Ponyta 20%, Stunky, Charcadet, Onix, Bonsly, Nacli, Salandit, Joltik, Dwebble, Frogadier 1%; Fletchinder and Stunky by day, Stunky and Houndour at night. A dry gully with lizards and scorpions, which is right. Frogadier at 1% under the Cycling Road, with no water on the map, is a leftover; Nacli is filler from the caves.

Proposals:
- 8%: Nacli out, Machop in. Vanilla's route is 20% Machop; Machop is placed (Jubilife, Route 207, Route 210), the same stage and slightly stronger (305 against 280), and Nacli keeps eighteen other zones.
- 1%: Frogadier out, Kricketune in. Vanilla's 10% resident, placed at Sendoff Spring, and as a rare slot it is a similar catch (384 against 405).

Confidence: high.

### Wayward Cave

The place: the cave under the Cycling Road, an upper maze and a hidden basement where Gible lives. Vanilla: Geodude, Bronzor, Zubat, Onix above; Bronzor, Geodude, Gible, Onix, Zubat below.

Fit: 1F (Carbink 28%, Bronzor 25%, Zubat, Geodude, Onix, Dwebble, Nacli, Bonsly) is a jewel-and-stone cave and fits. B1F (Phanpy 28%, Sandygast 25%, Glimmet, Gible 10%, Stunky, Hippopotas, Larvitar 1%, Nacli; Mightyena and Meditite by day, Houndour and Zubat at night) keeps Gible as the basement's prize, a good Larvitar at 1%, and Hippopotas next to Gible is a nice Sinnoh pairing. Sandygast at 25%, a beach sand-heap ghost, is in a cave with no sand and jars; the dark-type dogs are fine in a dark basement.

Proposals:
- B1F 25%: Sandygast out, Dwebble in. Dwebble is placed on 1F and lives in rock; at Lv 18-19 both are first stages of similar strength (325 against 320) and Dwebble evolves at 34, Sandygast at 42, so neither reaches its final form in the split. Sandygast keeps Route 213 and the Ruin Maniac's cave, where sand is real.

Confidence: high.

## Maylene split (level cap 38)

### Route 209

The place: the long route from Hearthome to Solaceon, cut by a stream, with the Lost Tower and the Hallowed Tower on it; all six tables are one capture. Vanilla: Bibarel, Roselia, Staravia, Ralts, Chansey; Gastly and Zubat in the tower.

Fit: the route (Gothita 20%, Budew 20%, Ralts, Fletchinder, Minccino, Glameow, Smoliv, Koffing, Tangela, Duskull, Meditite; Murkrow and Yamask at night) reads as a road to a graveyard, with Gothita's stare and the night ghosts doing the work. The five tower floors change floor by floor (Yamask and Koffing at the bottom, Misdreavus and Duskull under the fog at the top) and hold Klefki, Dhelmise and Sandygast as odd relics; since the whole route is one capture, the floors are flavour, and it is good flavour. Fletchinder is the run's everywhere-bird by now.

Proposals:
- 10%: Fletchinder out, Staravia in. Staravia is vanilla's Route 209 bird; the Starly line is placed on the first four routes and then vanishes from Sinnoh, which is its own region's bird. Same stage, about the same strength (340 against 405 for Fletchinder, both evolving in the mid-thirties).

Confidence: high.

### Solaceon Town

The place: the farming town with the day-care and the ruins. No vanilla table.

Fit: Happiny 20%, Girafarig 20%, Lickitung, Trapinch, Mightyena, Mankey, Flaaffy, Chatot, Murkrow, Smoochum, Tropius; Chatot and Trapinch by day, Lickitung and Murkrow at night. Farm animals (Flaaffy, Girafarig, Lickitung, a day-care Happiny) with pests (Mankey, Mightyena): reads as a rural town. Smoochum at 1% is the only odd one.

Proposals:
- 1%: Smoochum out, Chingling in. Chingling is placed (Floaroma, Route 211), is vanilla's Solaceon-side chime, and both are 1% baby-stage catches of similar worth (Chingling reaches Chimecho at 20 in Oxide).

Confidence: medium.

### Solaceon Ruins

The place: the underground ruins beneath the town, chambers spelling FRIEND, where vanilla puts only Unown.

Fit: Dwebble 20%, Geodude 20%, Klefki, Carbink, Nosepass, Bronzor, Yamask, Phanpy, Onix, Sinistea, Glimmet; Nosepass and Carbink by day, Yamask and Gothita at night. Half of it says "ruins" well (Bronzor the ancient mirror, Yamask the tomb mask, Klefki, Sinistea, Nosepass the compass); the other half (Dwebble, Geodude, Phanpy, Onix) is the same cave filler as every other cave, and at 45% of the table it is what the player meets. Unown is the vanilla set piece and is useless in a nuzlocke, so leaving it out is right.

Proposals:
- 5%: Phanpy out, Lunatone in, and 5%: Onix out, Solrock in. Both are placed (Iron Island), both are ruin-and-meteor Pokemon in the later games' sense, and at 5% they replace two cave first-stages with two mid-strength one-stage catches (440 each) at Lv 22, about the worth of an Onix.

Confidence: medium.

### Route 210

- Night: Liepard out, Noctowl in.

Confidence: high.

## Barry split (level cap 71)

### Sendoff Spring

The place: the fog-bound basin around the fourth lake, reached by Rock Climb, with Turnback Cave and the Distortion World's threshold in it. Vanilla: Graveler, Bibarel, Staravia, Chingling, Dusclops; Golduck alone on the water.

Fit: Donphan 20%, Glimmora 20%, Crustle 20%, Dhelmise, Grapploct, Kricketune, Absol, Weavile, Heracross 1%, Pawmot 1%; Beautifly and Heracross by day, Absol and Weavile at night. Dhelmise, a drowned anchor, and the night's Absol and Weavile get the place; Grapploct, Kricketune, Beautifly, Pawmot and Heracross are a summer meadow's cast dropped into a foggy crater, and vanilla's one ghost (Dusclops) is gone. The water is in `water.md`.

Proposals:
- 10%: Grapploct out, Dusclops in. Vanilla's resident; the Duskull line is placed; 490 against 480 at Lv 52.
- 8%: Kricketune out, Chimecho in. Vanilla's Chingling slot; the line is placed; a wind chime in the fog for a cricket, 455 against 384.
- Day: Beautifly out, Staraptor in (vanilla's Staravia slot; the Starly line's adult).
- 1%: Pawmot out, Drifblim in. The Drifloon line is placed; a balloon lost in the fog as the rarity, 498 against 486.

Confidence: medium.

### Route 223

The place: the open sea north from Sunyshore to the Pokemon League, islets to steer around; water only. See `water.md`. Mantyke, Tentacruel, Kingdra and Wailmer are the deep sea and read as it.

Confidence: high.

### Victory Road

The place: the last cave, three floors of rock, steel and a river, up to the League. Vanilla: Graveler, Rhyhorn, Onix, Rhydon, Golbat, Steelix, Gabite on 1F; Steelix, Magneton, Graveler on 2F; Floatzel, Azumarill, Graveler below; Floatzel and Golbat on the water.

Fit: 1F (Rhyperior 33%, Crustle, Hawlucha, Golem, Hariyama, Mienshao, Absol 1%, Weavile 1%), 2F (Skarmory 28%, Garganacl, Hawlucha, Gliscor, Steelix, Absol), B1F (Steelix 33%, Bronzong, Garganacl, Probopass, Carbink, Klefki 1%): an endgame cave of final forms, the right tone, and the floors differ enough to feel like a climb. Vanilla's Gabite, the cave's famous rare, is missing although its line is placed; Hawlucha and Garganacl each hold two floors as filler.

Proposals:
- 1F 1%: Absol out, Gabite in. Absol keeps 2F and the night; a 1% Gabite at Lv 40-43 restores vanilla's rarity. Note on balance: Gabite reaches Garchomp (600) at 48, stronger than anything else in the cave, so this is the one proposal that raises a table's ceiling; at 1% it is a lottery, as vanilla's 5% was.
- 2F 20%: Hawlucha out, Golbat's adult Crobat in. Vanilla's cave bat; at Lv 42 Crobat is the stage and 535 against 500 the worth; Hawlucha keeps 1F.

Confidence: medium.

### Pokemon League

The place: the mountaintop plateau at the end of Route 223, with pools beside the League building; water only. See `water.md`. Its four tables are the same Milotic, Crawdaunt, Lanturn, Goldeen and Jellicent list as the lakes and the Coronet water; on a mountaintop pool the sea species jar, and nothing here says "the end of the road".

## Across the run

The cast arrives in three waves and then stops. Counting the first split in which a line can be met anywhere:

| Split | New lines |
|---|---|
| Roark | 69 |
| Gardenia | 34 |
| Fantina | 27 |
| Maylene | 20 |
| Wake | 10 |
| Byron | 13 |
| Candice | 2 (Delibird, Lapras) |
| Galactic | 1 (Heracross) |
| Volkner | 0 |

From Candice's split on, every table is the evolved forms of lines met by Byron. As world-building that is fine, the region matures with the player; under the dupes clause it is the run's weakest stretch, because a box of twenty-five captures makes most of Route 225, 226, 227, 228, 229, 230, 222, Victory Road and Sendoff Spring already owned, and the "capture" in each is whichever rare slot is left. My late-zone proposals therefore reach for the rarer relatives of placed lines (Staraptor, Noctowl, Pelipper, Machamp, Hippowdon, Piloswine, Abomasnow, Dusclops, Chimecho, Drifblim, Crobat, Gabite, Magmar, Mr. Mime, Lunatone and Solrock, Sudowoodo, Girafarig) so that the last third offers something new to a full box without touching the balance. The unplaced lines that would do this job better are listed under flags below.

Themes that repeat too often. The cave list first: Oreburgh Gate, the Mine, Ravaged Path, Wayward Cave, all five Coronet zones, Maniac Tunnel, the Ruin Maniac's cave, Solaceon Ruins, Iron Island and Victory Road, thirteen capture areas, draw from about fourteen lines (Zubat, Geodude, Nacli, Nosepass, Bronzor, Carbink, Onix, Dwebble, Glimmet, Makuhita, Phanpy, Mienfoo, Golbat, Meditite). Each cave has a good signature (Onix and Magby in the Mine, Gible under Wayward, Goomy in the flooded hall, Hippopotas in the Maniac Tunnel, Yamask and Klefki in the Ruins, Steelix and Metang on Iron Island), but the 10% slots under the signature are the same in all of them, and by the third cave of a split the capture is a dupe. Then the filler lines that follow the player everywhere: Purrloin and Liepard in twenty-three zones, Fletchling's line in nineteen, Nacli in nineteen, Phanpy in fifteen, Smoliv in fourteen, Zubat in fourteen, Makuhita, Geodude, Houndour and Bronzor in thirteen each. The Fire scrub (Route 207, 214, Fuego, 227, Stark) is five zones but spaced through the run and reads as a thread, not a repeat; the three ghost zones (Chateau, Lost Tower, Route 209) sit inside two splits and are distinct enough.

What is missing for long stretches. Sinnoh's own bird, Starly, is on the first four routes and then never seen again, and Staravia and Staraptor never appear, while a Kalos bird (Fletchling) takes every later slot; Bidoof appears in three zones and Bibarel never; Psyduck and Golduck, the lakes' vanilla residents, are 1% to 4% slots. Fairy has no land presence at all in Byron's split and one line (Azurill's) in Wake's; from the Amity Square Snubbull at Fantina to the Candice snow there is nothing. Steel is absent from Wake's land tables, Ice from Fantina to Byron on land (fine, the snow is coming), Dragon is Gible at 10% and then a 1% Goomy until Byron. None of these is a fault by itself, but Fairy is the type Oxide added and it is thin everywhere the super-wanted Ralts line is not.

Where the lists stop feeling like the place: Route 218 (a Galarian mime leading a strait), Turtonator on the fog paths (Route 210 north, Route 211 east), Sandygast underground (Wayward Cave, Coronet B1F), a Seel and a Smoochum on the dry Route 214, Slugma in the snow (Routes 216 and 217), Dubwool, Dolliv and Smoliv wherever there is a coast or a river (Routes 213, 221, 205 south), Grapploct, Kricketune and Beautifly at Sendoff Spring, Raichu on Route 230, a Dewgong on the Coronet mountainside, and, in the water, Chinchou, Lanturn, Sharpedo and Wailmer in every inland pond (see `water.md`).

Two things that are policy, not faults, and that a fresh reader should name once. Starter lines are wild lines here (Litten at 20%, Snivy at 15%, Treecko, Torchic, Rowlet, Squirtle, Mudkip, Froakie, Charmander, Fennekin, Sprigatito's line, Braixen, Grovyle, Dartrix), which is consistent and gives the early routes a distinctive look; it does mean a Kalos starter is the commonest 1% in the game (the Froakie line is in thirty-five water zones). And the region reads as Galar and Alola laid over Sinnoh: Wooloo, Rookidee, Blipbug, Nacli, Smoliv, Pawmi, Sewaddle, Purrloin and Minccino are the early filler where Bidoof, Starly and Kricketot were. Where an import lands in its own biome it works at once (Frosmoth and Snom in the snow, Sandygast and Clobbopus on the beach, Dewpider in the marsh, Salandit and Turtonator on the volcano, Toucannon on the islet, Goomy in the flooded hall); where it is used as filler (Nacli, Smoliv, Dubwool, Purrloin) it flattens the map. The rule I would apply is one home biome per import and at most one neighbouring zone.

Per Ian's answer these are not proposals. Each is a line I would have reached for first.

- Magikarp and Gyarados: Lake Valor's drained bed is Sinnoh's one canonical Magikarp scene, and the line is absent from the whole game. Gyarados is also the nuzlocke community's standing S-tier water pick, which may be why; if so the absence is right and this flag is only a note.
